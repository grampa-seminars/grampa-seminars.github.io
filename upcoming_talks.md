## The Effective Kerr-Compton Tree Amplitude from Black Hole Perturbation Theory
Speaker: Konstantinos Papadimos
Date: 9 November 2026, 14:00 CET

Tree-level scattering amplitudes involving two massive external legs and an arbitrary number of gravitons serve as fundamental building blocks for higher-loop calculations of classical gravitational two-body dynamics. While amplitudes describing minimally coupled point particles are well understood, extending this framework to compact objects such as black holes requires incorporating additional physical information about their finite-size effects and dissipative properties.
In this talk, focusing on the gravitational Compton amplitude of a spinning black hole, I will discuss how black-hole perturbation theory can be used to provide the additional physical input needed to determine these contact terms, by matching the partial-wave decomposition of the effective field theory amplitude to gravitational-wave scattering in Kerr spacetime. I will outline the key ingredients of this matching and present results through sixth order in spin, including dissipative contributions associated with black-hole absorption. Finally, I will discuss consistency checks and potential applications to higher-order calculations of classical gravitational observables.
