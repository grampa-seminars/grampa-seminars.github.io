## Supertranslations from soft dressings
Speaker: Asaad Elkhidir (IHES)
Date: 11 May 2026, 14:00 CEST

States of a massive particle in electrodynamics and gravity are not well-defined because of the absence of a mass gap. In this talk, we examine some implications of this ambiguity within the KMOC formalism. Specifically, we study the associated freedom in the definition of the creation and annihilation operators of the massive particle.

We find that a class of choices modify classical observables in these theories, and that this modification is precisely a large gauge transformation. We identify the canonical and intrinsic BMS frames with the asymptotics of the Schwarzschild metric in Kerr-Schild and de Donder coordinates respectively, and study the consequences of large gauge transformations for gravitational waveforms and the impulse.

## Five-dimensional geometry from amplitudes
Speaker: Lucile Cangemi
Date: 27 April 2026, 14:00 CEST

Spinor-helicity variables neatly capture the little-group structure that constrains scattering amplitudes. In four dimensions, they've been a powerful tool for studying amplitudes involving higher-spin fields and gravitons, and can also be related to linearised gravitational solutions. In this talk, I'll explain how this framework extends to five dimensions, building on explicit constructions by Chiodaroli et al. and Pokraka et al. A key difference is that five dimensions allow a wider range of spinning states beyond the totally symmetric ones familiar from four dimensions. I'll discuss how this richer structure changes the kinds of classical solutions these amplitudes can describe.

## Mathematical formalism and geometric structure of twisted Feynman integrals
Speaker: Jungwon Lim
Date: 13 April 2026, 14:00 CEST

We introduce a class of deformed Feynman integrals, termed twisted Feynman integrals, in which the integrand is modified by an exponential factor linear in the loop momenta. Such structures arise in several contexts, including generating functions for tensor integrals, Fourier transforms, and spin-resummed dynamics in post-Minkowskian gravity.

In this talk, I will present a framework that makes the underlying geometric structure of these integrals manifest and discuss how standard tools can be extended to this setting. In particular, I will show that the Symanzik polynomials become graded rather than homogeneous, that twisted integrals belong to the class of exponential periods, and that their function space is not captured by leading singularities in the Baikov representation.

## Gravitational waves meet black hole perturbations
Speaker: Fabian Bautista
Date: 30 March 2026, 14:00 CEST

In this talk, we review the modern effective field theory approach to precision computations of gravitational-wave templates for binary black hole mergers. In particular, we emphasize the characterization of finite-size effects, such as tidal Love numbers, horizon dissipation, and spin effects, for black holes in both four and five dimensions.

Although finite-size effects are expected to contribute subdominantly to binary waveforms, they may nevertheless become measurable with future gravitational-wave observatories. For black holes, the corresponding effective coefficients are obtained through a matching computation between the effective point-particle description and the ultraviolet-complete solutions provided by black hole perturbation theory.

We also review a reformulation of these perturbative results in terms of Nekrasov–Shatashvili functions.

## Self-force EFT: weak-to-strong field resummation in black-hole binaries
Speaker: Riccardo Gonzo (QMUL)
Date: 23 March 2026, 14:00 CET

We discuss an effective field theory for point particles modeling black holes within the gravitational self-force (small mass ratio, or GSF) expansion, including spin effects. Using both diagrammatic background-field methods and traditional self-force techniques, we analyze the resulting 1SF metric perturbations and radiative observables. We then show that orbit-averaged observables such as the energy loss exhibit characteristic logarithmic divergences near the separatrix curve between scattering and plunging orbits, signalling the presence of a non-perturbative Regge cut in the strong-field. Finally, exploiting this analytic structure, we construct resummed expressions that interpolate between perturbative weak-field results (Post-Minkowskian and Post-Newtonian expansions) and strong-field (GSF) dynamics.

## Unlocking higher spin effects in black hole and neutron star binaries with supersymmetry
Speaker: Johannes Pirsch
Date: 24 November 2025, 14:00 CET

Combining first and second quantized degrees of freedom in the form of worldlines and gravitons allows for an extremely efficient representation of the two-body problem in General Relativity. This has been leveraged for example in the GWL and WQFT approach for solving the scattering problem of black holes and neutron stars.

An elegant approach for including the spin of compact objects on the worldline arises from the classical description of particles with fixed spin s. They are naturally described on the worldline by a N=2s supersymmetric action, where spin degrees of freedom are carried by anticommuting vectors. In this representation the classical spin tensor is composite, and the spin supplementary condition arises as a gauge fixing condition, automatically guaranteeing its conservation. It has however long been recognized that such models are hindered by a no-go theorem in curved space, prohibiting the description of s>1 particles. This automatically limits the multipole expansion of the classical dynamics to quadrupolar order (i.e. S^2) and is therefore not sufficient for reaching the precision required for future gravitational wave detectors.

In this talk, I will present the recently developed non-minimal coupling approach for supersymmetric worldline models. I will show how non-minimal coupling allows us to evade the no-go theorem and how it leads to fascinating results for higher spin Hamiltonians of compact objects.

## Dynamical multipole moments of spinning black holes
Speaker: Trevor Scheopner (AEI Potsdam)
Date: 11 November 2024, 14:00 CET

It is unknown what principles determine the EFT coefficients for a spinning black hole at high orders in the PM expansion and in spin. This question is equivalent to asking what are Dixon's multipole moments for a spinning black hole. This is in turn equivalent to asking what is the implied effective dynamical source of a spinning black hole. In General Relativity, there are two distinct notions of multipole moments, the Geroch-Hansen moments of a stationary field at infinity, and Dixon's multipole moments of a generic compact source. The Geroch-Hansen moments are fixed by three point amplitudes while Dixon's moments depend on and determine the full interacting dynamics of the body. I will review the distinction between these moments and then analyze possible proposals for Dixon's moments for a spinning black hole.

## Spinning black hole scattering at O(G^3) using fixed spin theories
Speaker: Dogan Akpinar (Edinburgh)
Date: 30 September 2024, 14:00 CEST

We resolve subtleties in calculating the post-Minkowskian dynamics of binary systems, as a spin expansion, from massive scattering amplitudes of fixed finite spin. In particular, the apparently ambiguous spin Casimir terms can be fully determined from the gradient of the spin-diagonal part of the amplitudes with respect to S^2 = −s(s + 1)ħ^2, using an interpolation between massive amplitudes with different spin representations. From two-loop amplitudes of spin-0 and spin-1 particles minimally coupled to gravity, we extract the spin Casimir terms in the conservative scattering angle between a spinless and a spinning black hole at O(G^3 S^2), finding agreement with known results in the literature. It turns out that the finite part of the amplitude coincides with the two-body radial action in the aligned spin limit, generalizing the amplitude-action relation beyond the spinless case. Surprisingly, the two-loop amplitude displays a hidden spin-shift symmetry in the probe limit, which was previously observed at one loop. We conjecture that the symmetry holds to all orders in the coupling constant and is a consequence of integrability of Kerr orbits in the probe limit at the first few orders in spin.

## Vanishing of nonlinear tidal Love numbers of Schwarzschild black holes
Speaker: Massimiliano Riva
Date: 15 April 2024, 14:00 CEST

## Absorption from scattering amplitudes
Speaker: Rafael Aoude
Date: 30 October 2023, 14:00 CET

In this talk, I will discuss how to model gravitational absorption effects using effective on-shell scattering amplitudes. ## Bethe-Salpeter equation and boundary to bound dictionary for generic Kerr orbits
Speaker: Riccardo Gonzo
Date: 29 May 2023, 14:00 CEST

The synergy between traditional general relativity tools and new scattering amplitude techniques has led to significant advances in our understanding of the behavior of gravitational systems. For the classical two-body problem, a dictionary is required to translate between results valid for hyperbolic orbits to the relevant elliptic ones, for example using a suitable analytic continuation. We begin by discussing the solution of the Bethe-Salpeter recursion relation for classical amplitudes in the HEFT formalism, making contact with the Hamilton-Jacobi action which describes the two-body dynamics for spinless and aligned-spin particles. ## Kerr black holes from higher-spin theory
Speaker: Paolo Pichini
Date: 15 May 2023, 14:00 CEST

Recent years have seen a surge of interest in massive higher-spin amplitudes, motivated by applications to Kerr black holes and gravitational wave physics. A special class of amplitudes, known as "minimally-coupled", were used to obtain state-of-the-art results for Kerr binary systems. However, only the three-point amplitudes are known in full generality and the four-point Compton amplitudes, needed for higher-order calculations, are still missing. In this talk, we ask ourselves the question: what makes the "minimally-coupled" amplitudes special, and how can it help us find the Compton amplitudes? To answer this, we study the constraints on massive higher-spin theories that are required to preserve the correct number of degrees of freedom. A systematic way to account for such constraints is to write down gauge-invariant Lagrangians, with the help of auxiliary Stückelberg fields. However, constructing the Lagrangians is no simple task, with only a few examples studied in the literature at cubic order and no explicit result at higher orders. We propose a new realisation of gauge invariance directly at the level of on-shell scattering amplitudes, in the form of novel massive Ward identities. At cubic order, we show that the "minimally-coupled" black-hole amplitudes are the unique lowest-derivative solution to the Ward identities, combined with a known unitarity constraint. At quartic order, we use the same methods to compute new Compton amplitudes and discuss how to use them to extract higher-order black-hole observables.

## Dissipative tidal effects of a Kerr black hole using effective worldline theory
Speaker: Muddu Saketh
Date: 5 December 2022, 14:00 CET

The mass and angular momentum of black holes in binary systems may change due to the infall of gravitational-wave energy down the horizon. For spinning black holes, this starts at 2.5PN with respect to the leading order energy flux at infinity. There has been a discrepancy in the literature in the expressions of these horizon fluxes in the test-body limit at 4PN order (1.5PN relative). We model the horizon-related dissipation as tidal heating in an effective worldline theory of a spinning particle equipped with tidally-induced quadrupole and octupole moments. We resolve the discrepancy in the test-body limit finding agreement with results obtained earlier using black-hole perturbation theory, where the tiny (test) mass is treated as a source perturbing the Kerr metric.

## Two tales about black holes
Speaker: Béatrice Bonga (Radboud)
Date: 28 November 2022, 14:00 CET

This is a two-part story about what gravitational-wave observations can tell us about the source of the object emitting these waves and their environments. Part one is about the gravitational multipole moments and the fact that they do not conclusively determine the nature of the object itself. In particular, I will show an explicit construction of several physically motivated Newtonian objects with multipole moments identical to those of Kerr. Part two is about resonances due to the interaction of two black holes orbiting a central massive black hole. Such tidal resonances are observable with future space-based gravitational wave detectors such as LISA and in principle allow us to extract information about the environmental tidal field of the EMRI system, albeit at the cost of a more complicated EMRI waveform model. ## High-spin scattering at 2PM
Speaker: Kays Haddad
Date: 31 October 2022, 14:00 CET

Describing the scattering of two Kerr black holes using scattering amplitudes requires knowledge of the opposite-helicity Compton amplitude to all orders in spin. The correct amplitude is known up to fourth order in spin, while the Compton amplitude constructed from BCFW recursion on the Kerr three-point amplitude develops spurious poles above fourth order in spin. In this talk, I demonstrate the removal of these poles for all spin orders in the classical limit, without affecting the factorization properties of the amplitude. This produces a viable Compton amplitude which can deviate from the true Kerr amplitude only by contact terms. I analyze the contact terms that can be relevant for black hole scattering at 2PM, and fix a large portion of them by assuming low-spin properties of Kerr scattering hold to higher spin orders. Finally, this allows for a closed-form, all-spin expression of the 2PM amplitude between a spinning body (which may or may not be Kerr) and a Schwarzschild black hole.

## Supersymmetric massive gravity
Speaker: Laura Johnson (Engelbrecht)
Date: 31 October 2022, 14:00 CET

Enhancing the symmetry of physical theories to include supersymmetry has a history of leading to important insights, and so we are investigating whether supersymmetry has interesting things to teach us about massive gravity. In this talk, we start by introducing massive spin-2 theories and reviewing some of their key features. Then, using massive spinor helicity variables, we review an on-shell superspace formalism for massive particles in four dimensions. Finally, we apply this formalism to massive spin-2 amplitudes, deriving all spin-2 cubic vertices that are compatible with supersymmetry and exploring the constraints that adding more supersymmetry has on these vertices. We conclude by commenting on computing the 4-point massive supersymmetric spin-2 amplitudes and other possible future directions.

## Frame-dragging effects in scattering amplitudes
Speaker: Jung-Wook Kim

In general relativity, the gravitational field generated by a spinning body causes immersed test bodies to co-rotate with the central body, which is also known as the frame-dragging effect. ## Double copy structures in axion-dilaton spacetimes
Speaker: David Peinador Veiga
Date: 28 March 2022, 15:00 CEST

In its original formulation, the double copy is a relation between scattering amplitudes in gauge theory and gravity. This representation of the relation is perturbative, in momentum space and on-shell. However, we know that certain classes of classical solutions exhibit exact double copy relations in position space, in contrast with the amplitudes formulation. In this talk, I will address this puzzle by showing how classical double copy relations can be obtained from the classical limit of the BCJ double copy for three-point amplitudes. In particular, I will focus on the axion-dilaton Kerr-Taub-NUT family of solutions, finding that the resulting Weyl spinors exhibit double copy relations in momentum space. Finally, in some cases with additional symmetry, the momentum space classical double copy relation also takes a local form in position space.

## Black-hole scattering and radiation at high energy
Speaker: Carlo Heissenberg
Date: 14 March 2022, 15:00 CET

In this talk, using amplitude-based methods, I will discuss some dissipative effects in the dynamics of two colliding black holes, and their interplay with radiative observables.
