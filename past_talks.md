## Supertranslations from soft dressings
Speaker: Asaad Elkhidir (IHES)
Date: 11 May 2026, 14:00 CEST

States of a massive particle in electrodynamics and gravity are not well-defined because of the absence of a mass gap. In this talk, we examine some implications of this ambiguity within the KMOC formalism. Specifically, we study the associated freedom in the definition of the creation and annihilation operators of the massive particle.

We find that a class of choices modify classical observables in these theories, and that this modification is precisely a large gauge transformation. We identify the canonical and intrinsic BMS frames with the asymptotics of the Schwarzschild metric in Kerr-Schild and de Donder coordinates respectively, and study the consequences of large gauge transformations for gravitational waveforms and the impulse.

## Five-dimensional geometry from amplitudes
Speaker: Lucile Cangemi
Date: 27 April 2026, 14:00 CEST

Spinor-helicity variables neatly capture the little-group structure that constrains scattering amplitudes. In four dimensions, they've been a powerful tool for studying amplitudes involving higher-spin fields and gravitons, and can also be related to linearised gravitational solutions. In this talk, I'll explain how this framework extends to five dimensions, building on explicit constructions by Chiodaroli et al. and Pokraka et al. A key difference is that five dimensions allow a wider range of spinning states beyond the totally symmetric ones familiar from four dimensions. I'll discuss how this richer structure changes the kinds of classical solutions these amplitudes can describe.

## Mathematical formalism and geometric structure of twisted Feynman integrals
Speaker: Jungwon Lim
Date: 13 April 2026, 14:00 CEST

We introduce a class of deformed Feynman integrals, termed twisted Feynman integrals, in which the integrand is modified by an exponential factor linear in the loop momenta. Such structures arise in several contexts, including generating functions for tensor integrals, Fourier transforms, and spin-resummed dynamics in post-Minkowskian gravity.

In this talk, I will present a framework that makes the underlying geometric structure of these integrals manifest and discuss how standard tools can be extended to this setting. In particular, I will show that the Symanzik polynomials become graded rather than homogeneous, that twisted integrals belong to the class of exponential periods, and that their function space is not captured by leading singularities in the Baikov representation.

## Gravitational waves meet black hole perturbations
Speaker: Fabian Bautista
Date: 30 March 2026, 14:00 CEST

In this talk, we review the modern effective field theory approach to precision computations of gravitational-wave templates for binary black hole mergers. In particular, we emphasize the characterization of finite-size effects, such as tidal Love numbers, horizon dissipation, and spin effects, for black holes in both four and five dimensions.

Although finite-size effects are expected to contribute subdominantly to binary waveforms, they may nevertheless become measurable with future gravitational-wave observatories. For black holes, the corresponding effective coefficients are obtained through a matching computation between the effective point-particle description and the ultraviolet-complete solutions provided by black hole perturbation theory.

We also review a reformulation of these perturbative results in terms of Nekrasov–Shatashvili functions.

## Self-force EFT: weak-to-strong field resummation in black-hole binaries
Speaker: Riccardo Gonzo (QMUL)
Date: 23 March 2026, 14:00 CET

We discuss an effective field theory for point particles modeling black holes within the gravitational self-force (small mass ratio, or GSF) expansion, including spin effects. Using both diagrammatic background-field methods and traditional self-force techniques, we analyze the resulting 1SF metric perturbations and radiative observables. We then show that orbit-averaged observables such as the energy loss exhibit characteristic logarithmic divergences near the separatrix curve between scattering and plunging orbits, signalling the presence of a non-perturbative Regge cut in the strong-field. Finally, exploiting this analytic structure, we construct resummed expressions that interpolate between perturbative weak-field results (Post-Minkowskian and Post-Newtonian expansions) and strong-field (GSF) dynamics.

## Unlocking higher spin effects in black hole and neutron star binaries with supersymmetry
Speaker: Johannes Pirsch
Date: 24 November 2025, 14:00 CET

Combining first and second quantized degrees of freedom in the form of worldlines and gravitons allows for an extremely efficient representation of the two-body problem in General Relativity. This has been leveraged for example in the GWL and WQFT approach for solving the scattering problem of black holes and neutron stars.

An elegant approach for including the spin of compact objects on the worldline arises from the classical description of particles with fixed spin s. They are naturally described on the worldline by a N=2s supersymmetric action, where spin degrees of freedom are carried by anticommuting vectors. In this representation the classical spin tensor is composite, and the spin supplementary condition arises as a gauge fixing condition, automatically guaranteeing its conservation. It has however long been recognized that such models are hindered by a no-go theorem in curved space, prohibiting the description of s>1 particles. This automatically limits the multipole expansion of the classical dynamics to quadrupolar order (i.e. S^2) and is therefore not sufficient for reaching the precision required for future gravitational wave detectors.

In this talk, I will present the recently developed non-minimal coupling approach for supersymmetric worldline models. I will show how non-minimal coupling allows us to evade the no-go theorem and how it leads to fascinating results for higher spin Hamiltonians of compact objects.

## Spinning the probe in Kerr with WQFT
Speaker: Jitze Hoogeveen (Humboldt University)
Date: 10 November 2025, 14:00 CET

Classical and QFT techniques for binary black hole scattering are highly complimentary, and may inform each other. In the self-force (SF) regime, classical position-space methods may significantly streamline weak-field QFT approaches. Inspired by this synergy, we formulate the worldline quantum field theory (WQFT) approach in position space. Focus is on the simple 0SF probe limit. Here, a position space treatment enjoys significant advantages over momentum space. With a custom IBP procedure, we compute scattering observables up to 7PM. Remarkably, the iterated integrals involved fully factorise at all loop orders considered. We conclude by discussing possible applications to the SF expansion.

## Coherent states, background fields, and double copy
Speaker: William Lindved
Date: 27 October 2025, 14:00 CET

We show that scattering amplitudes on any gauge theory background admitting a coherent state description double copy to amplitudes in a curved spacetime. The metric of the spacetime is built from the gauge background using a notion of classical double copy which emerges naturally at the amplitude level. In the self-dual sector this map relates backgrounds which are exact vacuum solutions in gauge theory and gravity.

## Classical gravitational Compton amplitudes from flat and curved space
Speaker: Carl Jordan Eriksen (NBI)
Date: 29 September 2025, 14:00 CEST

Motivated by the study of extreme mass-ratio binary systems, the use of curved backgrounds in computations of classical, gravitational amplitudes have recently appeared in the literature [2308.15304, 2308.14832]. While these investigations concern the self-force expansion, the use of curved backgrounds is interesting in its own right. In this talk, I discuss how one can use worldline quantum field theory and an expansion around the Schwarzschild–Tangherlini metric to reformulate the perturbative expansion of the Compton amplitude, which describes the scattering of a graviton off a compact object. Specifically, I address the curved computation of the first and second post-Minkowskian Compton amplitudes, and their consistency with flat space calculations.

## Unexpected symmetries of Kerr black hole scattering
Speaker: Graham Brown
Date: 15 September 2025, 14:00 CEST

Recent years have seen considerable progress in computing scattering observables for spinning black holes at high PM orders, using both amplitude based and worldline methods. In this talk we will use these results to investigate the integrability properties of scattering Kerr black holes, both in the probe limit and beyond. We will begin by reviewing the radial action and the recently introduced Dirac bracket formalism, before applying them to define a notion of asymptotic integrability. Using these tools, we will show that this integrability holds to higher orders in spin than previously known. Finally, we will give a new perspective on spin shift symmetry and show how this can be combined with integrability constraints to help bootstrap the radial action.

This talk is based on arXiv:2508.10761.

## Inelastic coupled-channel eikonal scattering
Speaker: Matteo Sergola
Date: 2 June 2025, 14:00 CEST

Emitted radiation and absorption effects in black hole dynamics lead to inelastic scattering amplitudes. In this paper, we study how these effects introduce an inelasticity function to the 2→2 eikonalised S-matrix and how they can be described using unequal mass and spin on-shell amplitudes. To achieve this, we formulate the inelastic coupled-channel eikonal (ICCE) using the KMOC formalism and the language of quantum channels, where off-diagonal channels involve mass and spin changes.

## Amplitudes for Hawking radiation
Speaker: Rafael Aoude
Date: 19 May 2025, 14:00 CEST

In this talk, I will show an approach to compute Hawking radiation based on on-shell scattering amplitudes. The Hawking spectrum is obtained by exponentiating a series of Feynman diagrams describing a massless scalar field scattering through a collapse background. Using semiclassical methods, we obtain an in-in generalisation of an amplitude closely connected to the Bogoliubov coefficients. Finally, I will show how subdominant one-loop correction can be interpreted as finite-size corrections sensitive to the radius of the black hole.

## Beyond spin in orbital dynamics
Speaker: Dimitrios Kosmopoulos
Date: 5 May 2025, 14:00 CEST

What are the degrees of freedom that describe a spinning object? In this talk, I will present a quantum field theoretic framework for spinning objects that extends the conventional description based solely on a spin vector. This generalization arises naturally from a little group analysis and introduces an additional vector degree of freedom, leading to a richer multipolar structure. I will discuss how observables are affected by this extra degree of freedom and present two examples of systems that exhibit it: a Newtonian bound system of two particles, and a black hole in a specific matter-coupled gravitational theory.

## Self-force from self-energies in worldline effective field theory
Speaker: Gustav Jakobsen
Date: 28 April 2025, 14:00 CEST

Self-force and radiation reaction effects are crucial for gravitational wave phenomena. Fundamentally, the radiation of waves by compact objects, such as black holes or neutron stars, entails a loss of energy and momentum of the objects carried away by the waves. Most simply, in classical electromagnetism, this effect is described by the Abraham-Lorentz-Dirac (ALD) equation. In this talk, I will discuss how this equation arises in worldline effective field theories through self-energy diagrams using the diagrammatic worldline quantum field theory approach. This framework also allows for a systematic inclusion of finite-size effects and I will discuss spin and susceptibility contributions to the ALD equation. Having understood the simple example of electromagnetism, I will turn to gravity and discuss radiation effects in the post-Minkowskian expansion and their importance for resummation and comparisons against numerical relativity.

## Effective field theory in a mass ratio expansion
Speaker: Nabha Shah (NBI)
Date: 25 November 2024, 14:00 CET

Field theoretic approaches, perfectly tailored for computations perturbative in Newton's constant, have had great success in producing state-of-the-art results for the gravitational two-body problem. On the other hand, metrics such as the Schwarzschild metric and geodesic trajectories in these spacetimes carry information to all orders in the gravitational constant and can be interpreted as resummations of infinite classes of Feynman diagrams. In this talk, I will describe recent work that seeks to leverage the all-orders-in-G information encoded in classical solutions to simplify post-Minkowskian computations. Dynamics beyond the leading order in mass ratio are described by an effective field theory with a systematic mass ratio expansion. The EFT naturally provides "recoil operators" that encode the back-reaction caused by the effect of the test body on the background.

## Dynamical multipole moments of spinning black holes
Speaker: Trevor Scheopner (AEI Potsdam)
Date: 11 November 2024, 14:00 CET

It is unknown what principles determine the EFT coefficients for a spinning black hole at high orders in the PM expansion and in spin. This question is equivalent to asking what are Dixon's multipole moments for a spinning black hole. This is in turn equivalent to asking what is the implied effective dynamical source of a spinning black hole. In General Relativity, there are two distinct notions of multipole moments, the Geroch-Hansen moments of a stationary field at infinity, and Dixon's multipole moments of a generic compact source. The Geroch-Hansen moments are fixed by three point amplitudes while Dixon's moments depend on and determine the full interacting dynamics of the body. I will review the distinction between these moments and then analyze possible proposals for Dixon's moments for a spinning black hole.
